# Ryan Trahan Video Format Playbooks

Complete structural templates for each of Ryan's major content formats.

---

## FORMAT 1: THE PENNY/RESOURCE CHALLENGE SERIES

**The OG format.** Start with an absurdly small resource and trade/hustle/barter it into something massive — all while raising money for charity.

### Core Variables
- **Starting resource**: one cent, one object, one meal
- **End goal**: reach a location, trade to a car, raise $X for charity
- **Twist mechanic**: the "Great Reset" — donations or failures force a reset to zero
- **Duration**: multi-episode daily series (best), or single long video

### Episode Structure (Daily Format)
```
[TEXT: Day X — [Resource Amount]]
Cold open: Show current resource status + daily mission
Morning: First attempt / first trade / first obstacle
Midday: Setback or unexpected development
Evening: Either progress or vulnerability beat
End card: Tease tomorrow + running charity total
```

### The Great Reset (Essential Plot Device)
- A rule violation, large donation, or failed trade resets progress
- Should happen at least once per series
- Must create genuine stakes (viewer groans, not eye rolls)
- Recovery from reset is where Ryan's character shines most

### Series Arc
- **Week 1**: Establishment + early wins + audience falling in love with premise
- **Week 2**: The Great Reset + lowest point + doubt moment
- **Week 3**: Recovery + momentum building + character moments with strangers
- **Final Days**: The push to the finish + Grand Payoff reveal
- **Final Day**: Charity total reveal + emotional reflection

### Charity Integration
- State the charity in Episode 1 clearly
- Show the donation link/goal every episode
- The charity total IS a character — it rises, dips (after resets), rises again
- Final reveal should include real-time footage if possible

## FORMAT 2: THE BUDGET CONSTRAINT CHALLENGE

**Structure**: Experience something on an absurdly small budget. Often single video.

### Variations
- "I Survived on $X for Y Days"
- "I Did [Expensive Thing] for $1"
- "I Tried to [Achieve Goal] with $0"

### Script Structure
```
HOOK (0:00–1:00)
- Show the constraint immediately (bank account, cash, penny)
- State the experience/goal
- Establish what "success" looks like

SETUP (1:00–3:00)
- The rules (simple, slightly impossible)
- The personal stakes (why Ryan is doing this)
- First planning montage (often self-deprecating)

ACT 1 — The Early Days (3:00–7:00)
- First obstacle (always harder than expected)
- First creative solution (surprisingly clever)
- First character interaction (future Kevin?)

ACT 2 — The Struggle (7:00–11:00)
- Midpoint crisis (running out of resource, being denied, hitting a wall)
- Vulnerability beat (genuine doubt)
- Unexpected help from a stranger (community = warmth)

ACT 3 — The Resolution (11:00–14:00)
- The final push
- Does he succeed? (Ryan is honest — failure is possible and valid)
- What he learned (NOT a TED talk — one or two sentences max)

OUTRO (14:00–15:00)
- Charity CTA if applicable
- Tease next video
- Grand Payoff reveal if applicable
```

## FORMAT 3: THE N-DAYS IMMERSION CHALLENGE

**Structure**: Spend X days living inside a game, experience, or constraint. Full arc.

### Best Examples
- "I Spent 100 Days in Grand Theft Auto"
- Any "100 Days in [world]" format

### What Makes This Work
1. **The world has rules** that create natural conflict
2. **Character evolution** — Ryan at Day 1 vs Day 100 is a different person
3. **Supporting cast** (Kevin-style NPCs or real people) create emotional anchors
4. **The three-act decline or growth** — who is Ryan becoming?

### Script Blueprint
```
HOOK (three-act preview)
- Show Day 100 footage briefly (where this ends up)
- Cut back to Day 1 (we're starting from scratch)
- Plant the question: "How does a person end up [at Day 100 state]?"

ACT 1 — Days 1–33: The Idealist
- Ryan has a noble goal
- Early wins come easily
- He's doing it "the right way"
- End of Act 1: First major obstacle that requires compromise

ACT 2 — Days 34–66: The Pragmatist
- Rules become negotiable
- Character adaptation (he's becoming someone slightly different)
- Kevin moment — a character who reflects Ryan back at himself
- End of Act 2: The point of no return — he crosses a moral line

ACT 3 — Days 67–100: The Reckoning
- He achieves the goal
- But what did it cost?
- Reflection moment (genuinely philosophical, not cheesy)
- Return to Day 1 footage — who was that guy?
```

## FORMAT 3.5: THE 50 STATES / EPIC TRAVEL SERIES (Ryan's Most Ambitious Format)

**The pinnacle format.** 50 consecutive daily episodes, each in a new US state, raising money for St. Jude Children's Research Hospital. Raised $11–12 million total.

### What Made It Work: The TV Show Formula
Ryan deliberately engineered this series to feel like a **comfort TV show** — families watched it together every evening as a ritual.

**The template that never changed:**
```
ARRIVAL IN STATE (establishing shot, state name/shape in thumbnail)
AIRBNB REVEAL — honest ranking of the accommodation
STATE-SPECIFIC EXPLORATION — what makes this state interesting
WHEEL OF DOOM (if triggered) — wild card challenge
DONOR MESSAGE READING — personalized moment for contributors
FUNDRAISING UPDATE — running total shown prominently
TEASE TOMORROW — what state is next
```

### The Recurring Identity Elements (Cartoon Character Consistency)
- **Same outfits every single episode** — like a cartoon character's signature look. Creates visual continuity and comfort. Viewers anticipate the outfit, not the wardrobe.
- **Same intro music cue** — emotional Pavlov trigger. Hearing it = "it's Ryan time."
- **The Wheel of Doom suitcase** — a physical object that appears in every episode. Fans recognize it instantly. It's a character.
- **State shape/name in every thumbnail** — signals to regional viewers "this is YOUR state episode"

### The Gamified CTA (Donation Tier System)
This is Ryan's most sophisticated CTA design — donors don't just give money, they **buy narrative agency**:

| Donation Tier | What Happens |
|---|---|
| $5,000 | On-air shoutout by name |
| $50,000 | **Triggers the Wheel of Doom** — donor affects the story |
| $100,000+ | **Donor chooses the challenge** for that episode |

This transforms passive viewers into active story participants. The CTA IS the plot.

### Recurring Daily Rituals (Penny Series)
In the penny series specifically, Ryan built **mundane daily rituals** into the structure:
- McDonald's coffee as the daily morning anchor
- Teeth brushing shown as a recurring marker of "new day"
- "Jammy Time" penalty (50 States) — wearing pajamas all day for certain failures — becomes a recurring comedic element viewers anticipate

### Brand Integration (The Lectric Bikes Model)
Rather than ad reads, sponsors became **narrative characters**:
- Lectric bikes were used to actually travel between states
- The sponsorship became organic B-roll content — "here's me riding the Lectric bike through [state]"
- Never framed as a paid placement; framed as "the tool I'm using for this journey"

### Community as Protagonist
The 50 States series created something unusual: **the audience organized independently**. A Strava running group formed among fans who ran miles as fundraising units. The community became a character in the story without Ryan orchestrating it. Write for this possibility — give the audience something to DO beyond watching.

## FORMAT 4: "I STAYED IN EVERY X" TIER PROGRESSION

**Structure**: Work through every tier of something (hotels, restaurants, airlines) from cheapest to most expensive.

### The Appeal
- Accessible aspiration (viewers live vicariously)
- Built-in comparison comedy
- Natural escalation creates momentum
- Grand Payoff = the top tier reveal

### Script Structure
```
HOOK
- Establish the spectrum (cheapest vs. most expensive)
- The secret mission (what Ryan is doing at the top that nobody knows)
- The constraint (budget, rules, time limit)

EACH TIER = MINI EPISODE
- Establishing shot of the tier
- Ryan's honest reaction (never snobbish about the cheap, never fawning about the expensive)
- The unexpected discovery (cheap tiers often have surprises; expensive tiers sometimes disappoint)
- One character interaction per tier minimum
- End of tier: numerical comparison (price, amenity count, square footage)

ESCALATION COMEDY
- The contrast between tiers is the joke
- Ryan's reaction to price difference ("This costs...more than my first car")
- Found-object comedy (what does the $30 hotel have that the $3000 hotel doesn't?)

GRAND PAYOFF (Top Tier)
- Maximum production value description
- The secret plan revealed
- Emotional beat (Ryan calls Haley, gives charity announcement, or personal milestone)
```

## FORMAT 5: THE SOCIAL EXPERIMENT / WORLD RECORD

**Structure**: Attempt something measurable and unusual. Document the attempt fully.

### Best For
- "I Broke [World Record] in [Unusual Category]"
- "I Tried to [Do Thing] in Every [Location] in America"
- Social experiments with strangers

### Script Structure
```
HOOK
- What is the record/experiment?
- Why does it exist?
- What is Ryan's approach?

THE RULES (strict, verifiable)
- How success is measured
- What counts and what doesn't
- Who is judging (Guinness, Ryan himself, the internet)

THE ATTEMPT MONTAGE
- Fast cuts through multiple attempts
- Failure compiled = comedy
- Progress compiled = inspiration

THE NEAR-MISS MOMENT
- Almost succeeds
- Something goes wrong
- Stakes genuinely feel real

THE FINAL ATTEMPT
- Slowest editing in the video
- Let the moment breathe
- Ryan's face reaction = the reward for viewers who stayed

AFTERMATH
- Verification process
- What happens next
- What Ryan actually thinks about having done this
```

## UNIVERSAL ENDINGS (Ryan's Outro Styles)

### The Charity Reveal Ending
> "[Challenge] is over. Here's what we did together."
> [Show total raised, footage of charity impact]
> "Thank you. For real. I mean that."
> [Ryan, quiet, not performing emotion]

### The Personal Reflection Ending
> "Day 1, I thought this was going to be about [the surface goal]."
> "It turned out to be about [the deeper thing]."
> [One specific moment from the journey]
> "So yeah. That's the video. See you next time."

### The Grand Payoff Ending
> [Secret plan executes]
> [Silence for 2–3 seconds]
> [Ryan looks at camera]
> "I've been waiting 30 days to do that."

### The Honest Failure Ending (Ryan is willing to lose)
> "I didn't make it."
> [Show final count/distance/result]
> "But here's what happened instead."
> [Reframe: what the failure taught him, or what good came from it anyway]
