# Ryan Trahan: Voice, Script Patterns & Language Guide

---

## Core Voice Principles

Ryan sounds like he's telling you a story at lunch, not presenting to a camera.

**The three-word summary**: warm, awkward, driven.

Everything else flows from those three words.

---

## Sentence Structure Patterns

Ryan writes in short bursts punctuated by longer contextual sentences.

**Pattern A — The Punchline Setup**:
> Short setup. Shorter setup. [Punchline that recontextualizes both.]

Example:
> "The plan was genius. Foolproof, even. I had forgotten one thing: I'm not a fool."

**Pattern B — The Rhetorical Beat**:
> [Situation]. [Reaction question]. [Answer that raises stakes.]

Example:
> "So I'm standing in a Walmart parking lot with 37 cents. What do you do with 37 cents? You negotiate a car, apparently."

**Pattern C — The Internal Monologue Drop**:
> [Narrated action]. My brain: [absurdist thought in brackets or italics].

Example:
> "The hotel concierge asked if I had a reservation. My brain: tell him you're a YouTuber. My mouth: 'I have... ambition?'"

**Pattern D — The Self-Aware Parenthetical**:
> [Statement] — which, for me, [ironic qualifier].

Example:
> "Day 3 was the hardest day of my life — which, for me, says a lot, because I once spent a week trying to eat only food from vending machines."

## Recurring Structural Phrases

Use these freely — they're Ryan's verbal DNA:

### Setup/Stakes Phrases
- "The rules are simple." (then a complex rule)
- "Here's the thing about [X]..."
- "So I had a choice."
- "This changes everything."
- "Okay. So."
- "Wait. Wait wait wait."

### Reaction Phrases
- "So that happened."
- "Classic me."
- "Which, in hindsight..."
- "I want you to know that I thought this was a good idea."
- "My brain at this moment: [thought]"

### Transition Phrases
- "Day [X]."
- "Hour [X]."
- "[Dollar amount] left."
- "Okay, so let's rewind."
- "Here's where it gets interesting."
- "I need to be honest with you."

### Connection Phrases (breaking fourth wall)
- "I know what you're thinking."
- "You're not going to believe this."
- "Stick with me."
- "We're getting ahead of ourselves."
- "Let's start from the beginning."

### Vulnerability Phrases
- "I genuinely didn't know if I could do this."
- "I'm not going to pretend that wasn't scary."
- "At this point I'm starting to panic."
- "I've never done anything like this."

## Humor Style: Self-Deprecation as Connection

Ryan's humor NEVER punches at others. It punches at himself.

**The Self-Deprecation Formula**:
> [Describe his confident plan] + [Reveal how it goes wrong] + [Lean into the embarrassment]

Example:
> "My trading strategy was legendary. I had a whole spreadsheet. Color-coded. Multiple tabs. I showed up to the first trade and the guy just... didn't want to trade. He just wanted to talk about his dog. So I talked about his dog for 45 minutes. The spreadsheet did not have a tab for this."

**What he doesn't do**:
- Mock strangers' appearances or intelligence
- Exaggerate for shock value
- Punch down at people who help him

**What he does do**:
- Mock his own overconfidence
- Exaggerate his own anxiety or awkwardness
- React to genuinely absurd situations with deadpan observation

## The Vulnerability Beat

Every major Ryan Trahan video has at least one moment where Ryan genuinely admits doubt, fear, or exhaustion. This is NOT performed vulnerability — it reads as real.

**How to write it**:
- Short sentences
- Present tense (even in retrospect)
- Physical grounding (he notices something mundane)
- No resolution yet — the doubt just sits there

Example:
> "Day 15. I'm sitting in a Denny's at 11pm. My phone has 4% battery. I've made $3.47 today. The goal is $1 million. I eat my pancakes. I don't know if I can do this."

## The "Kevin" Character Technique

Named for the NPC in "100 Days in GTA" who became the audience's favorite character.

**The formula**:
1. First mention: one line of dialogue that's oddly specific or funny
2. Second mention: reference the earlier line, acknowledge the callback
3. Third mention (optional): give them a meaningful moment that pays off the investment

**Rules**:
- Never make them the joke — they're the heart
- The audience has to LIKE Kevin before Kevin's payoff moment
- Kevin's return should feel like running into an old friend

## Pacing Blueprint (15-Minute Video)

| Minute | Energy | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:00 | High | 6-step hook |
| 1:00–3:00 | Medium-high | Rules, setup, character intro |
| 3:00–6:00 | Medium | First obstacles, early progress |
| 6:00–8:00 | Spike | First major setback or twist |
| 8:00–10:00 | Low | Vulnerability beat, reflection |
| 10:00–12:00 | Rising | Recovery, momentum building |
| 12:00–14:00 | High | Climax sequence |
| 14:00–15:00 | Emotional low then high | Grand Payoff reveal, reflection |

## Sponsor Integration (Ryan's Style)

Ryan doesn't hide sponsorships but he makes them feel natural:

- Brief: 30–45 seconds max
- Self-aware: acknowledges it's an ad
- Often tied to the challenge: "Speaking of surviving on a budget, this video is sponsored by..."
- Personal: he actually uses the product, or admits he's skeptical but tried it
- Humor: one self-deprecating joke about himself or the transition

Example sponsor read:
> "Okay before we get to Day 15, I need to tell you about Squarespace, which is sponsoring today's video. Now I know what you're thinking. 'Ryan, you're trading a penny across America — what do you need a website for?' And to that I say... fair point. But you might need one. Link below. Code RYAN for 10% off. Okay back to the penny."
