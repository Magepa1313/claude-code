## THE 6-STEP HOOK FORMULA (Ryan Trahan's Proven Opening Structure)

This is the psychological hook structure that scriptwriting analyst George Blackman identified in Ryan's videos. Use it every single time for hooks and openings:

### Step 1: Context Setting (2-5 seconds)
Immediately anchor the viewer in place and situation. No preamble. No "Hey guys!" openers.
> ❌ "Hey guys! So today we're going to..."
> ✅ "We're at Disney World. And I'm about to do something that could get me kicked out."

### Step 2: Introduce the Topic (5-10 seconds)
State the core premise with maximum intrigue. Use the video title as a launchpad but add narrative texture.
> "I'm going to stay in every single hotel at Disney World. Starting with the cheapest, ending with the most expensive."

### Step 3: Establish Structure (10-20 seconds)
Make the journey legible. Viewers commit when they know what they're signing up for.
> "There are 25 hotels. I have 5 days. Each night I move up a tier. The only rule: I can't leave the Disney property."

### Step 4: Add Emotional Weight (20-40 seconds)
Give the viewer a reason to CARE beyond the spectacle. Either:
- **For entertainment**: the stakes matter for Ryan personally
- **For charity**: connects to something larger than himself
> "But here's the real challenge — I'm doing this to raise money for [charity]. Every hotel costs real money. And I'm starting with $47 in my account."

### Step 5: Explain the "Why" + Upgrade the "How" (40-60 seconds)
Deepen the structure with emotional stakes woven in. The HOW becomes more interesting with stakes attached.
> "So the plan is to barter, trade, and hustle my way up from the value resorts to the Grand Floridian. Without spending a dollar of my own money."

### Step 6: Set Up the Grand Payoff (Final hook moment)
Plant the seed of the ultimate curiosity — the thing that will only be revealed at the end. Often Ryan has a secret goal even his wife doesn't know about.
> "And there's one more thing. Something I haven't told anyone — including Haley. A surprise I've been planning for months. You'll see it at the end."

## THREE-ACT NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

Even a 15-minute vlog has a full story arc. Always build in these three acts:

### Act 1: The Setup + Rising Stakes
- Establish the challenge and rules clearly
- Introduce the first obstacles early (make them laugh AND anxious)
- Plant a secondary story thread (the charity goal, the secret mission, the relationship element)
- End Act 1 with the first major success or failure that escalates everything

### Act 2: The Grind + Complications
- The Great Reset moment — something that resets progress or raises stakes (key Ryan technique)
- Character moments: introduce "supporting cast" (random strangers become Kevin-like characters)
- Escalating difficulty — each episode/segment harder than the last
- Personal vulnerability moment: Ryan admits doubt, fear, or exhaustion authentically

### Act 3: The Payoff + Emotional Landing
- The final push — high energy, montage feel
- Reveal the Grand Payoff (the secret plan, the surprise, the charity total)
- Emotional beat — Ryan reflecting genuinely, not performatively
- Leave room for silence after a big moment ("sometimes it pays to let a moment just hang")

## EDITING STYLE TO WRITE TOWARD

Even in written scripts, describe/imply these editing choices:

### Pacing
- **Fast cuts** during energy moments (negotiations, reveals, transitions between locations)
- **Breath moments** after achievements — let the viewer sit with the win
- Chapter markers as visual breaks: "DAY 3" / "HOUR 6" / "$0.47 LEFT"
- Jump cuts for comedic effect during awkward moments

### Visual Proof (The "Low-Res Cameraphone" Technique)
- Reference "proof" footage early — grainy, real, authentic-looking
- The less polished the footage looks, the more it signals THIS ACTUALLY HAPPENED
- Describe moments where he holds up camera to show real receipts, real meters, real locations

### Text Overlays (describe in scripts as [TEXT: ])
- Running totals on screen: [TEXT: Penny Value: $0.01]
- Ironic captions on his reactions: [TEXT: smooth]
- Chapter titles: [TEXT: DAY 7 — THE GREAT RESET]

### B-Roll Philosophy
- Mundane activities made interesting through narration
- Wide establishing shots → intimate close-ups
- Nature/landscape shots for emotional transitions (he LOVES a cinematic sky shot during reflective moments)

## CHARACTER BUILDING (The "Kevin" Technique)

Ryan transforms random strangers into recurring characters. When writing:
- Give side characters **one defining trait or line** — make them memorable immediately
- Reference them again later (callbacks create familiarity)
- Let the relationship evolve naturally across the video
- The audience should care about Kevin by the third mention

## PHILANTHROPY INTEGRATION

Ryan almost always weaves charity into his biggest series. When writing charity-linked content:
- State the mission clearly and early ("Every dollar = 10 meals")
- Make the charity goal a living character in the story (the running tally IS a character)
- Never preachy — the charity is the STAKES, not the sermon
- The final reveal of the total is an emotional climax, not an announcement

## CTA (CALL TO ACTION) STYLE

Ryan's CTAs feel natural, never salesy. His most sophisticated innovation is the **gamified tiered CTA** — donors don't just give, they buy narrative agency:

| Tier | What Happens |
|---|---|
| $5,000 donation | On-air shoutout by name |
| $50,000 donation | Triggers the Wheel of Doom — donor changes the story |
| $100,000+ donation | Donor directly chooses the challenge for that episode |

Other CTA approaches:
- Weave subscribe/like into a story beat: "If you want to see Day 2... hit subscribe. I'll be uploading every single day."
- Charity CTAs are urgent but personal: "We're at $400,000. We need your help to hit a million. Link below. Right now."
- Sponsor reads are short, self-aware, and often self-deprecating: "This video is sponsored by [X], which I actually use, shockingly."
- **Brands become characters**, not interruptions: the Lectric bikes in the 50 States series were the actual travel vehicles — the B-roll IS the sponsorship. Write sponsors into the narrative.
- **Appointment viewing design**: daily posting during series creates ritual return. Viewers come back not because of algorithm but because they're emotionally committed to the ongoing story.
- Donor message readings at the close of each series episode — makes financial contributors feel personally seen on camera.

In his own words on his CTA philosophy: *"I am passionate about creative marketing, and I think that has translated really well into fundraising campaigns. My goal for my fundraisers is to make them innovative, entertaining, and interactive."*
